# Syllabus notes

Source: the official PRV 201 syllabus ("PRV 201 — Frontiers of Technology (AI)",
three pages), received on 2026-09-29 and kept locally as `course/PRV201_Syllabus.pdf`
(git-ignored: this repository is public and the syllabus carries the instructor's
contact details). What follows restates the parts the project uses; quotations are
marked. Earlier versions of this file (until 2026-09-29) were a stand-in built from
the project brief.

## Course outcomes (quoted)

- **Scientific or Technological Literacy** — "Students identify and explain
  fundamental AI and machine learning concepts, system components, and application
  areas, and analyze how different AI approaches operate. They evaluate each
  approach's capabilities and limitations and attribute observed system behavior to
  its underlying technical mechanisms."
- **Computational Ability** — "Students define a real-world problem and select
  appropriate AI/ML approaches and tools to address it. Across the project's
  milestones, they design and build a working solution or analysis and evaluate its
  output against the problem they defined."
- **Collaboration and Professional Practice** — "Students plan and execute a
  multi-milestone AI project in interdisciplinary teams. They formulate the problem to
  be addressed, evaluate peer teams' work and incorporate that feedback into their
  own, and justify their design decisions to a professional audience."
- **Ethics** — "Students analyze and evaluate ethical issues arising from AI systems,
  including bias, privacy, transparency, and accountability. They apply ethical
  frameworks to case studies and formulate reasoned judgments about the responsible
  design, deployment, and use of AI technologies."

## Course objectives (summary)

The course builds technological and informational literacy, ethical reasoning, global
awareness and leadership readiness. Students analyze AI across high-impact domains,
including regulatory, governance and societal considerations, and "analyze current
industry developments, including technological breakthroughs, regulatory actions and
investment trends", evaluating "the strategic, economic and leadership implications of
AI adoption across sectors" through recurring engagement with industry news. The team
project gives direct experience "scoping, building and presenting an AI system or
analysis".

## Grading

Quizzes 25% (80 points: 10 points x 8 quizzes); team project milestones 75%.

| Milestone | Weight | Points |
|---|---|---|
| M1 - Problem statement | 8% | 25 |
| M2 - Design / approach | 14% | 45 |
| M3 - Prototype / draft | 20% | 64 |
| M4 - Peer review | 7% | 22 |
| M5 - Demo day / capstone | 26% | 84 |

The point totals match `rubric/derived_rubric.md`. The syllabus gives no per-line
rubric, so the derived line items stay in use (ADR-001, ADR-038).

## Important notes (summary)

- Assignments listed in a given week are due by the start of the following class.
- Every milestone is graded once as a team, then individually. The team splits each
  milestone into as many parts as it has members and each student leads and signs
  one part: a section of the write-up for M1-M3, a subset of the shared rubric's
  criteria (applied across both reviewed teams) for M4, and a slice of the capstone
  deliverable or presentation for M5. A student earns 100% of the team's score for a
  full, meaningful signed part, 50% for a partial or non-meaningful part and 0% for no
  contribution.
- For every milestone an AI model gives a preliminary read of each rubric line before
  a human grader reviews the submission and enters the score that counts; the AI's
  read is a reference only. Before that read, an automated step strips names, emails
  and other identifying details.
- Attendance is mandatory, with deductions after four absences.

week 11, M4 at week 13, and the final milestone and videos (non-finalists) at the end
of week 13; finalists present in week 14.
