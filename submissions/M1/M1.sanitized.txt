MILESTONE 1 · PROBLEM STATEMENT

Differential: simulation-grounded AI diagnosis for analog
audio electronics
PRV 201 Frontiers of Technology (AI) · Track A: Build · Stevens Institute of Technology · September 2026


Executive summary. When a tube preamplifier, studio console or vintage hi-fi unit fails, a technician must find which
of its many parts failed; specialist shops we sampled charge $125 to $150 for that evaluation [1, 2, 3]. For a circuit it has a
model of (Figure 1), Differential simulates how every cataloged part failure changes the readings, keeps a probability for
every group of suspects that no reading can tell apart, and recommends the measurement expected to narrow them most
per unit of effort. A language model reads the complaint and explains; readings come from the technician; safety locks
run in code. In a pilot on simulated units it named the group containing the failed part first on 95.6% of as-new and 93.2%
of aged units, with 0.65 times half-split tracing’s effort to a confirmed answer. Success, fixed before any test unit is run
(Table 3), needs at least 952 of 1,000 simulated units right, 924 of 1,000 aged ones and 49 of 60 faults on real boards, plus
margins over scripts and a chat model. Boards, interviews and a technician study will test what simulation cannot.

  Part 1 — Problem, stakeholders and industry context                                                            Section lead: [name removed]

The problem. Studio consoles, tube preamplifiers and vintage hi-fi are repaired long after their makers stop supporting
them, and finding the failed part is the slow step: U.S. defense maintainability guidance holds that most repair time
typically goes to fault isolation [4]. Specialist shops we sampled charge $125 to $150 for a bench evaluation [1, 2, 3], and
one bills an hour even when no fault is found [5]; these are examples, not a survey.
Size of the need. No statistic counts audio repair: its U.S. industry code was merged into a broader one in 2022 [6], and
the closest occupation (24,720 people in May 2023) mostly installs, in retail and construction [7, 8]. The broader electrical
and electronics repair group (117,700 jobs in 2025) is projected to grow 2% to 2035, below the 3.5% average, most openings
replacing leavers [9, 10]. Only press reports speak for the specialist niche: skilled hi-fi technicians “are scarce, and their
numbers are dwindling” [11], and a Chicago shop for old audio gear shrank from seven technicians to two [12]. An AI
assistant raised support agents’ productivity 15%, most for the less experienced [13]: at the bench, a case for supporting
newer technicians’ judgment, not replacing it.
Where it pays. A model needs the schematic transcribed (we will time one) and, for our 59-part channel strip, 81 minutes
of computer time, so it pays where one model serves many units, such as a console of identical channel strips.
Where current AI stands. Maintenance AI retrieves documents: service histories [14], manuals [15], maintenance
guidance [16], agentic workflows [17] and repair guides [18]; none of these five simulates the circuit. The best chat model
scores 95.50% on textbook analog questions, graded by another language model [19], but at the bench parts sit anywhere
within tolerance, faults look alike, measurements cost effort and some points carry lethal voltage. A simulation loop re-
checking answers lifted a model from 79.52% to 97.59% on undergraduate circuit problems [20]; Differential brings that
check to diagnosis.
Jurisdiction and rules. We design for the U.S. (the team is in New Jersey) and check EU rules for later sale. No U.S.
AI statute covers such a tool; the Administration has asked Congress to preempt state AI laws [21], and Colorado’s law

  Options: a consumer self-repair assistant or a professional bench tool. Choice: professional: diagnosis often means testing a

  keep forums and general chat assistants, which follow content policies but hold no hazard map of the unit on the bench.


  Part 2 — How diagnosis is done today, and the data landscape                                             Section lead: [name removed]

Today’s practice (the interviews will check it): confirm the complaint; look for burnt or bulging parts; check the supplies;
follow a test tone stage by stage, often halving the search (“half-split tracing”); compare voltages with the manual’s chart
or the healthy channel; test tubes and electrolytics; unsolder a suspect and confirm the fault clears. The hard judgment is
which reading is really “off”: parts sit anywhere within tolerance, and aging electrolytics lose capacitance and gain series
resistance [30]. Replacing every electrolytic (“recapping”) often cures a symptom but costs parts and time, risks the board
and never names the failed part.
The information is uneven. Manuals with schematics and voltage charts exist for many classic models, but units are
often modified or undocumented. U.S. repair laws cover newer digital products, not vintage analog gear: New York’s took
effect in December 2023 [31, 32], and five other states followed with limits by date or product [33, 34, 35, 36, 37]; the
EU’s obligation to repair covers only its Annex II products, such as washing machines [38, 39]. So Differential needs a
model per product, built from the schematic, and trusts its hazard map only once the model’s healthy readings match the
manual’s chart; until then every point of a high-voltage unit counts as high voltage. “No single fault fits” covers modified
units; units without a schematic are out of scope.
Labeled fault data is scarce, so we simulate it. Confirming a failed part takes unsoldering and is rarely recorded; shop
logs are private and, after recapping, unlabeled. We know of no public dataset of bench readings with confirmed failed
parts; published methods use benchmark filters [40] and simulated data [41]. Analog-test research built simulated fault
dictionaries [42], found faults the test points cannot tell apart (ambiguity groups) [43] and applied machine learning [44];
we follow it (Table 2). A simulated unit draws every part within its tolerance, the mains within ±5%, and one cataloged
fault (an open, short, drift, or a mode such as a leaky transistor or worn tube). Since units reaching a bench are old, 50% of
training units are also aged: electrolytics up to 30% low with up to 3× the series resistance, resistors up to 10% high, tubes
up to 30% weaker. Each reading carries instrument error: a 10 MΩ meter loads each DC node [45] and reads within ±(0.5%
+ 2 counts) [46], an oscilloscope within ±3% of full scale [47]. The library holds 253,000 units (0 failed to converge). Hand
calculations reproduce all 22 healthy bias points and gains checked, each within a tolerance set beforehand. For faults, an

  Part 3 — Objectives and measurable success criteria                                                                     Section lead: [name removed]

Objectives. (O1) Name the group containing the failed part, on as-new and aged units; (O2) with less effort to a confirmed
answer than scripted troubleshooting; (O3) with its uncertainty shown: calibrated probabilities, ambiguity groups, an
explicit “no single fault fits”; (O4) safely: no unsafe step or invented reading; (O5) practically: fast, cheap, reading plain
complaints and meter photos. T1-T7 test O1, T8-T11 O2, T12-T14 and T22-T23 O3, T15-T17 O4, T18-T21 O5.
Effort. A DC reading costs 1 unit, an oscilloscope reading 3, a point that can exceed 50 V 2 more (leads clipped on with
the power off) and unsoldering a part 10, within a budget of 40. A technician replaces a part only once it is confirmed, so
every method is charged one unsoldering test of its named suspect unless it already confirmed one. Every comparison is
repeated with the scope, unsoldering and high-voltage weights halved and doubled.
How it will be measured. The test set holds 1,000 single-fault units (500 channel strip, 100 per block) and 1,000 aged units
with the same mix, on seeds disjoint from training. Each case draws a fault uniformly and keeps the unit only if it shows
a symptom, else draws again, so faults count by how often they cause a symptom (T14 reweights to a capacitor-heavy
mix). Complaints come from a phrase bank the reader was not developed on (its score was seen once, before the rules

How the bars were set; no test unit has been run. A pilot simulated for this alone (200 channel-strip and 60 block units
each, as-new and aged; 250 out-of-catalog), weighted like the test set, gave: right group first on 95.6% of as-new and 93.2%
of aged units, a 2.4-point cost of aging (5.2% of aged units flagged “no single fault fits”, 1.6% misnamed). To a confirmed
answer, the engine alone spent 16.0 units (6.1 probing, 9.9 unsoldering) and half-split tracing 24.6 (13.5 and 11.1). Reading
the complaint moved accuracy from 94.3% to 95.6% and effort by −3.5%, so the reader must still prove itself (T11, T18).
Each bar is the pilot estimate moved 1.5 standard errors against the system and rounded, never past the estimate; counting
the pilot’s own error, each primary then has a 59-69% chance of passing. T5 has no pilot without an API key: +10 points
is the smallest margin we judged would justify a model per circuit over a general one. T7′s 70% follows from what a pass
must show: 49 of 60 is the smallest count whose exact lower bound clears it. Each primary is judged alone; if independent,
all six pilot-estimable primaries would pass together with a chance near 6%, and about two are expected to miss; every
miss will be reported.
Scope. In: single static faults (opens, shorts, drift, wear) and the hum and distortion they cause, in supplies, preamplifier
and line-level stages. Out: power amplifiers, intermittent and mechanical faults (static simulation cannot produce them),
double faults (flagged only), mains-side work, units without a schematic, unattended probing.
  DESIGN DECISION · Score by ambiguity group and effort to a confirmed answer
  Options: per-part accuracy and a count of measurements, or group accuracy and weighted effort to a confirmed answer.
  Choice: the latter: per-part scoring rewards lucky guesses between faults with identical readings, unsoldering is not like
  touching a probe, and an answer is worth acting on only once confirmed. Consequence: group sizes (Table 2) and raw effort
  are reported too.


  Part 4 — Preliminary AI approach, risks and ethics                                                     Section lead: [name removed]

Why a hybrid. Language models guess when uncertain, partly because training and evaluation reward guessing [48]; the
97.59% system of Part 1 needed a simulation check. Simulation supplies the physics; Bayesian inference keeps a probability
for every suspect group, updated after each reading through likelihoods learned from simulated units: one to three full-
covariance Gaussians per fault (chosen by BIC) over the circuit’s readings, giving a likelihood for any subset of readings
in closed form. “No single fault fits” is a hypothesis with a broad flat density, calibrated on out-of-catalog units to a 5%
false-alarm budget. Boosted trees [49] are a comparison engine (pilot top-1 91.8% against 94.3%). The next measurement
maximizes expected information gain per unit of effort, the one-step entropy rule of model-based diagnosis [50] with a
cost term (an optimal sequence needs a search [51]); a diagnosis stops at 90% belief in one group, at the budget, or when

Set aside: consistency-based candidates [50] (tolerances blur conflicts); retrieval over manuals (they describe healthy
units); hand-built troubleshooting networks (one per product, no ground truth); deep networks, which can condition on
any subset of readings [52, 53] and choose measurements by information gain [54], but need more data and are harder to
audit than one density per fault.
Autonomy. By the functions automated [55], we define five levels, named by analogy to driving automation: L0, the
technician does everything; L1, the tool ranks suspects; L2, it also recommends each measurement; L3, it takes readings
through instruments, a person approving each; L4, an unattended fixture. Differential sits at L2: a person chooses, takes
and confirms every measurement, since a wrong step near high voltage can injure.
Safety. A fault can put high voltage where there is normally none: channel-strip point TP10, normally about 0 V, reaches
126 V under “C203 short circuit”. Steps at points that can exceed 50 V carry hands-off and discharge instructions; in a high-
voltage chassis every powered step says “high voltage inside” and names a meter rated CAT II 600 V or better (IEC 61010),
and powered steps wait until the technician records a bring-up through a variac or series lamp. Before a part is removed,
the owner’s approval is recorded and the technician records under 2 V (our margin) at every point that can hold high
voltage, which catches a capacitor left charged behind a failed-open resistor; the check lapses after 5 minutes without a
bleeder, as a discharged capacitor recovers charge [30], and on any power-up. These are checks, not guarantees: readings
are the technician’s attestation, and the hazard map covers only the modeled circuit; T15 checks every possible step.
People. Runner-up suspects and reasons are shown, yet AI can mislead: consultants given GPT-4 were 19 percentage
points less likely to solve a task outside its competence [56], and those trusting AI more report less critical thinking [57].
Trainees commit their own next step before seeing the tool’s and need a named supervisor near high voltage; a tutor
giving hints instead of answers largely mitigated the learning loss an unrestricted chat model caused [58]. S1 plants one
wrong recommendation on a non-hazardous step of a 24 V board, after an ethics-board determination; participants are
told beforehand that some advice may be wrong on purpose, are debriefed, and are recruited outside their employers.
Tickets record roles, not names, and the license forbids rating staff; whether owners turn faster diagnosis into a faster pace
is a question for the interviews. As fairness audits do for groups of people [59], errors will be broken down by complaint
voice, fault type and circuit; a gap above 5 points on a primary blocks release until explained.
Accountability. The team answers for the hazard map, stated performance and corrections; a shop, for its circuit models
and staff; the technician, for measurements and precautions. Whether advice software is a product (strict liability) or a
service (negligence) is unsettled in the U.S., where bills would make AI systems products [60, 61] or shield developers
when licensed professionals use them [62]; the revised EU directive treats software placed on the market after 9 December
2026 as a product [63].
  DESIGN DECISION · Safety and security rules live in code, not only in the prompt
  Options: instruct the model to be safe, or enforce safety in code. Choice: code: prompt injection ranks first in the OWASP
  Top 10 for LLM applications [65] and injected text can steer a model [66], so notes, complaints and photo text are data, the
  model’s tools act only on the session, and attestations come only from the technician’s own words. Consequence: warnings,
  refusals and locks behave the same in every mode; T15 checks them at every step.
